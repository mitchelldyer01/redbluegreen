Bandwidth

What the memory system gives a streaming kernel, measured with the vector add.

vadd is the simplest kernel that can fill the memory system: one work-item per element, two loads and one store, and no reuse of a loaded value. That makes it a probe of bandwidth rather than of arithmetic. The host times it from just before the doorbell write to the moment the completion signal reaches zero, so the number holds the launch as well as the kernel. Each size runs five reps with a fresh signal and reused buffers, and the best rep is kept.

Bandwidth counts twelve bytes per element over that best time. The two large sizes settle at 88 percent of the theoretical peak, which is as near as a streaming kernel gets here. The smallest streaming size falls short because the launch is still a large share of its total.

A dispatch of one element costs 133 us, and that is a floor under every dispatch rather than a property of memory. david has to amortize it. A training step must move enough bytes that the floor is small against the step, so the floor sets a lower bound on batch size.

Fine grained data runs at the same speed as coarse data on this load, so the COHERENT flag costs nothing while streaming. The largest fine buffer hangs before its first store and logs no fault. That stays an open item, and the data stays coarse.

Two of our own processes at once split the bandwidth between them instead of adding to it. With a local model server generating alongside, the reps ran at roughly half speed and every result still verified. The hardware runs the queues side by side and does not starve one for the other.

The earlier guess at the peak is now a measurement. The other guess, a few TFLOP/s of arithmetic, waits for the first matmul in unit 6.
